# Brand brief: Limestone (Vietnam)

Slug and repo: `vietnam`. Country: Vietnam (VN). Brand type: country. Brief v1, 2026-10-26. Facts below were checked on that date. Anything marked *verify* needs an official source before launch.

## 1. Name

**Limestone** (assigned, kept). The karst towers of Ha Long Bay, Lan Ha and Ninh Binh, and the caves of Phong Nha-Ke Bang, are all limestone: old seabed, lifted, then cut by rain and sea into the shapes on every Vietnam poster. It is one plain English word, easy to say and spell, and it sounds like a quiet, design-literate company. It also stands for the brand idea: Vietnam taken slowly, one landscape at a time, the way water works on stone.
- Search check: one web search (2026-10-26) found no tour or travel company trading as Limestone in Vietnam. The hits were Hanoi operators who mention "limestone scenery".
- Trademark: a human must search the **Intellectual Property Office of Viet Nam** (IP Viet Nam, https://ipvietnam.gov.vn, classes 39 and 43), plus **UKIPO**, **EUIPO** and **USPTO**. The domain and social handles are also a human task.
- Risks: a common word, so trademarking needs a distinctive logo and a class-39 filing. Search confusion with South Australia's **Limestone Coast** tourism region and with building-stone suppliers. The sibling Laos brand (Khaen) also uses karst imagery, so keep the two visually apart (see section 6).

Runners-up:
1. **Paddy**: plain English, the green of the deltas and terraces. Friendly, but it reads as an Irish first name and an Irish bookmaker.
2. **Sampan**: the small rowing boat of Trang An and the Mekong, a word most travellers know. Risk: several Vietnamese restaurants and cruise products already use it.
3. **Lotus**: Vietnam's best-known flower. Easy and warm, but heavily used (cars, software, hotels), so trademarking is near impossible.

## 2. Positioning

*For unhurried travellers: Vietnam's 1,600 kilometres taken one landscape at a time, with the right region for the month and the quiet bay instead of the busy one.*

The gap. UK and US specialists sell Vietnam as the north-to-south "classic" in two weeks, often bundled with Cambodia: Audley, Selective Asia (strong reviews), Remote Lands at the ultra-luxe end, Ampersand for luxury Asia. The products are good but the shape is the same: Hanoi, an overnight on Ha Long, Hoi An, Saigon, Mekong. Nobody owns a single-country edit organised around **landscape and season**: Lan Ha and Bai Tu Long instead of the crowded bay, the cave ladder in Phong Nha, the central coast in its dry months, and fewer bases with longer stays.

## 3. Audience

**A. The slow-travel couple (UK/US/AU, 50s–60s, 18–21 nights).**
- Motivation: wants the whole length of the country without the rush. Food, craft, architecture, quiet boats, a good guide in each region.
- Objections: worried about traffic, crowds in Ha Long and Hoi An, typhoons, and long internal transfers. "Is three weeks enough?"

**B. The active family or friends (London/Singapore/Sydney, 35–55, 12–14 nights).**
- Motivation: caves, kayaks, a jeep through Ha Giang, a cooking class with teenagers who actually eat.
- Objections: fitness levels vary in the group; they need to know which caves need swimming and which are boardwalks. Road safety.

## 4. Brand story (≈185 words)

> In Lan Ha Bay, before the day boats arrive, the water between the karsts is so still that a kayak leaves a line you can see for minutes. The stone is grey where the rain has washed it and black where it hasn't, with a skirt of green at the waterline where the sea has carved the foot away. Somewhere behind you a fisherman is pulling in a net by hand.
>
> We named the company after that stone because it is the thread through Vietnam. It rises out of the paddies at Trang An, where women row the sampans with their feet. It holds the caves of Phong Nha. It shapes the passes of Ha Giang. And it teaches the thing we try to plan around: everything here was made slowly.
>
> So we plan slowly. Vietnam runs about 1,600 kilometres from north to south, with three weather systems. We match the region to the month, choose the quiet bay over the famous one, and give each place enough nights to settle. Then we find the cook, the boatwoman and the caver who make it yours.

## 5. Voice

1. **Measured.** Calm sentences, no hype. Let the detail carry it.
   - Do: "Lan Ha, not Ha Long. Same stone, a tenth of the boats."
   - Don't: "Cruise the iconic emerald waters of mystical Ha Long Bay!"
2. **Seasonal.** Every recommendation names a month and a region.
   - Do: "Hoi An in October can flood. In March it is dry and the river is calm."
   - Don't: "Vietnam is perfect to visit year-round."
3. **Exact about effort.** Say how hard, how wet, how long.
   - Do: "Hang Tien is a full day with a short swim. Paradise Cave is a boardwalk."
   - Don't: "Embark on an epic adventure into the depths of the earth."
- Banned: exotic, timeless, mystical, Indochina charm, hidden gem, breathtaking, unforgettable, bucket list, "Good Morning Vietnam", war-tourism clichés, conical-hat silhouettes as decoration.

Sample headlines:
- Lan Ha, not Ha Long. Same stone, fewer boats.
- Three weathers, one country. We plan around all three.
- The cave ladder: from boardwalk to four days underground.
- Ninh Binh by sampan, rowed by foot.
- Three weeks, five bases, no rush.

## 6. Palette, fonts, design

Lead pairing: **limestone grey + lotus pink + paddy green.** The closest siblings: Laos (Khaen: paper, karst mist, indigo, Mekong silt) and Cambodia (Angkor Trails: sandstone, laterite, moss, lotus pink and dawn blue in time-of-day modes). Limestone has no indigo, sandstone or blue: its ground is a cool stone grey, its pink is a fixed lotus (no time modes), and its green is a wet young-rice green. "Slate + blush" is taken, so the grey here is light and mineral, never slate, and the pink is a strong lotus, never blush.

## 9. Licensing route (human decision)

- **In Vietnam:** inbound tour operators need an **international travel service licence** from the Vietnam National Authority of Tourism under the Law on Tourism 2017, with a bank deposit (https://vietnamtourism.gov.vn). Foreign-invested firms face limits on the services they can offer. The practical route is to contract a licensed Vietnamese inbound operator and record its licence number.
- **Selling to UK customers through a UK company:** ATOL for flight-inclusive packages (https://www.caa.co.uk/atol-protection/) plus insolvency protection under the Package Travel Regulations 2018.
- **US:** check seller-of-travel registration for California, Florida, Washington and Hawaii.

## 10. Top 3 risks

1. **Weather.** Typhoon season runs May to November and hits the centre hardest from about September to December (FCDO). Hoi An and Hue flood. FCDO has no advise-against areas for Vietnam (checked 2026-10-26), so the risk is seasonal: the planner must warn on central-coast dates in the storm months and the brand must say so plainly.
2. **Sameness.** Every operator sells the same north-to-south line. Which Bay, the Cave Ladder and the quiet-bay choices must lead, or Limestone becomes another Ha Long cruise seller. Son Doong sells out years ahead: never imply availability.
3. **The name.** "Limestone" is a common word (Limestone Coast in Australia, stone suppliers). IP Viet Nam and UKIPO class 39 are unchecked. Keep the Laos sibling's karst look distinct: no indigo, no river calendar.
